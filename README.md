## Part I — The col(F)/ker(F) Partition at Acoustic-Cultural Scale

The partition has been identified across 23+ disciplines (UNIVERSAL BOUNDARY, ERI Labs).

The acoustic-cultural analog has not been previously named. It is:

$$\text{col}(F_{\text{acoustic}}) = \{s \in S : P(\text{transmission}(s) \to \text{next generation}) > \tau\}$$

$$\text{ker}(F_{\text{acoustic}}) = \{s \in S : P(\text{transmission}(s) \to \text{next generation}) < \tau\}$$

where $S$ is the full space of song forms present in a community's archive and living practice, and $\tau$ is the intergenerational transmission probability threshold. Song forms in col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) are being actively transmitted; song forms in ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) are archived or remembered but not transmitted across generations.

The partition is directly measurable using the foundation model architecture described in HUMAN SONGS:

- ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) = $S$ \ col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$): the difference between archive and practice is the acoustic null space

The Northern Aché case defines ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) ⊃ {lullaby, communal dance} with empirical precision: 43 years of direct behavioral observation (Singh & Hill, 2025) established categorical absence in community practice while the biological capacity remains. The Warlpiri ρ₇ storybook (Mongabay, May 2026) is a partial encoding of col($F_{\text{Warlpiri, Songline}}$) into a digital substrate before the kernel expands irreversibly.

## Part II — The Dual-Channel MVCC: Hippocampal Spatial + FOXP2 Acoustic

The MVCC for spontaneous spatial recombination was formally characterized at four scales in SESAME COMMONS:

| Invertebrate | Bumblebee ball-rolling | {blue→reward} ∪ {ball→movable} |

HUMAN SONGS established FOXP2/corticostriatal as the vocal learning substrate shared between songbirds and humans. The synthesis identifies a fifth entry in the MVCC scale table:

| Scale | System | MVCC composition | Neural substrate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Individual vocal | Active song repertoire | Social-context/transmission conjunction | FOXP2/corticostriatal |

The social-context/transmission conjunction is precise. Research on FoxP2 in Area X (the song-dedicated striatal nucleus in zebra finches, anatomically homologous to human anterior striatum) shows that FoxP2 is dynamically regulated by social context:

- **Undirected singing** (singing alone, no receiver present): FoxP2 downregulated → vocal variability increases → ker($F_{\text{vocal, individual}}$) expands
- **Female-directed singing** (singing to a receiver): FoxP2 maintained at high levels → song stabilized → col($F_{\text{vocal, individual}}$) maintained

This is the individual-scale acoustic col(F)/ker(F) switch. Social engagement activates transmission mode (col dominant); isolation activates exploration mode (ker dominant). The colony of finches as a whole implements col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) as the set of songs that are being actively transmitted in social contexts. Songs sung only in isolation — increasingly variable, FoxP2-low — are at risk of ker expansion.

The Aché application: the FOXP2 circuit is biologically intact in every Northern Aché individual. No receiver models the lullaby in relevant social contexts (Singh & Hill, 2025: "It's not that the Northern Aché don't have any need for lullabies. Aché parents still calm fussy infants."). The FoxP2/corticostriatal circuit waits for the social input that activates transmission mode. The social input never comes. The lullaby is not in ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) because the individual lost the capacity. It is in ker because no social context activates the transmission mode.

**FORMAL STATEMENT OF THE DUAL-CHANNEL MVCC:**

$$\text{MVCC}_{\text{spatial}} \equiv \min\{|X| : I(a_{\text{novel, spatial}}; a_{\text{observed}} \mid X) > 0\}$$
$$\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;\text{implemented by: hippocampal place/grid system (Huber et al., 2025)}$$

$$\text{MVCC}_{\text{acoustic}} \equiv \min\{|X| : I(a_{\text{novel, acoustic}}; a_{\text{transmitted}} \mid X) > 0\}$$
$$\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\;\;\text{implemented by: FOXP2/corticostriatal system; socially activated}$$

The two channels operate in parallel. The bumblebee (Bhambore et al., June 2026) uses the spatial channel only: its MVCC is a what/where conjunction. The Northern Aché has the acoustic channel intact but its social activation condition is absent. The Songline uses both channels simultaneously.

**TH(dual-channel):** The vertebrate brain implements two parallel MVCC channels — spatial (hippocampal) and acoustic (FOXP2/corticostriatal) — both with col(F)/ker(F) structure, both regulated by social context, both subject to failure when social transmission density falls below the Allee threshold. The Songline is the only known cultural institution that has engineered redundancy across both channels simultaneously.

## Part III — The Lullaby as Acoustic Proto-MVCC

The cross-cultural acoustic regularities of infant-directed song have been established with precision. Mehr et al. (2021, *Nature Human Behaviour*) demonstrated cross-cultural stereotypy of infant-directed song acoustics across 21 societies: slower tempo, higher pitch, reduced accentuation, simple repetitive melodic contour. Lense et al. (2022, *PNAS*) demonstrated that the rhythmic structure of infant-directed singing entrains infants' social visual behavior — gaze toward the caregiver's face — establishing the lullaby as a multimodal conditioning object that simultaneously trains auditory, visual, and social attention.

The formal identification: **the lullaby is the acoustic proto-MVCC — the minimum viable conditioning clause from which all subsequent acoustic cultural Commons are built.**

This claim has three components:

**Component 1 — The lullaby is at the information-rate floor.** McBride et al. (2025) identified the Complexity Transmission Floor (T2 in HUMAN SONGS): the minimum information rate below which a melody loses its functional distinctiveness. The acoustic features of the universal lullaby — slow tempo, simple repetitive contour, high pitch, reduced accentuation — are precisely the features at the lower bound of the McBride et al. information-rate constraint. The lullaby is not merely simple. It is the lowest-complexity song form that retains functional distinctiveness as an acoustic cultural object. It sits exactly at T2 — the floor of the Complexity Transmission Floor.

**Component 2 — The lullaby seeds the FOXP2/corticostriatal acoustic MVCC.** Infant auditory experience shapes tonal expectations — the implicit probability distributions over pitch sequences that govern musical perception — during the first decade of life (Trainor & Trehub, 1992). The lullaby is the first acoustic input to the FOXP2/corticostriatal system. It seeds the initial X_{t-1} of the acoustic MVCC at the individual developmental scale. Every subsequent acoustic cultural conditioning event builds on this seed. A child who receives no lullabies enters later social song learning without an acoustic proto-MVCC: the FOXP2 circuit is functional but its initial X_{t-1} is empty.

**Component 3 — Loss of the lullaby predicts loss of higher acoustic conditioning layers.** The Northern Aché lost both communal dance and infant-directed song (Singh & Hill, 2025). The co-extinction is not coincidental. The lullaby seeds the acoustic MVCC; communal dance requires a populated X_{t-1} to function. This is the MVCC cascade: when the acoustic proto-MVCC is in ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$), the conditioning clauses that depend on it — all higher acoustic cultural behaviors — are at heightened risk. The inverse Bhambore effect: the bumblebee has sufficient $X_{t-1}$ and generates a novel recombination. The Aché child has $X_{t-1, \text{acoustic}} \approx \emptyset$ and cannot generate the acoustic cultural behaviors that depend on prior conditioning.

**TH(lullaby, proto-MVCC):** The lullaby is the acoustic analog of the bumblebee's two-clause training. It is the minimum viable conditioning clause for all subsequent acoustic cultural Commons. Communities where ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) includes the lullaby are at risk not merely of losing infant-directed song but of losing the acoustic proto-MVCC on which all higher acoustic cultural layers depend. The Aché case is the first documented instance of this cascade in a living human population.

## Part IV — The Three-Phase MVCC Lifecycle: Emergence, Maintenance, Collapse

SESAME COMMONS established the scale-free invariance of the MVCC. CANTIO established the register-crossing maintenance of the devotional conditioning Commons across 4,300 years. HUMAN SONGS established the collapse mechanism through the Northern Aché case. The synthesis identifies a three-phase lifecycle that none of the source programs named:

**Phase 1 — Emergence.** Novel behavior generated from two or more partial conditioning clauses that have never been combined before. Formal condition: $X_{t-1} \neq \emptyset$, $|X_{t-1}| \geq |\text{MVCC}|$, novel solution state $a_s \notin \{a_1, \ldots, a_{t-1}\}$.

*Canonical instance:* Bhambore et al. (June 2026). The bumblebee is given $X_1 = \{\text{blue} \to \text{reward}\}$ and $X_2 = \{\text{ball} \to \text{movable}\}$. The novel solution $\text{move}(X_2) \to \text{reach}(X_1)$ is in col($F_{\text{recombine}}$). The session is minutes long. The brain weighs 0.001 grams.

**Phase 2 — Maintenance.** The conditioning Commons is kept alive across substrate changes, generational transitions, geographic dispersal, and institutional disruption through register crossings — active transitions from one encoding substrate to another when ker($F_{\rho_n}$) becomes too large.

*Canonical instance:* CANTIO. The devotional conditioning Commons has been in active maintenance for 4,300 years across seven register crossings: Enheduanna's clay → Hebrew scrolls → Greek/Latin liturgy → Gregorian modal system → Lutheran vernacular compression → Watts/Wesley personal testimonial → Gospel embodied-global. Each crossing is forced by ker($F_{\rho_n}$) outgrowing the current substrate. Each crossing finds a new substrate before the Commons is lost. The Browning acid burns away what cannot survive the crossing; what survives is pure conditioning gain.

*Second instance:* Songline. 65,000 years of maintenance across at least six identified register crossings (ρ₁: local-acoustic → ρ₂: continental-protocol → ρ₃: legal-Commons → ρ₄: resistance-encoding → ρ₅: legal-recognition → ρ₆: digital-GIS → ρ₇: interactive-digital, 2025–present). The ρ₇ Warlpiri digital storybook (Mongabay, May 2026) is the current active maintenance event.

**Phase 3 — Collapse.** The biological MVCC substrate (FOXP2/corticostriatal for acoustic; hippocampal for spatial) survives but $X_{t-1}$ is reduced below the MVCC threshold by demographic or social disruption. col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) shrinks; ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) expands. If no register crossing is available — no new substrate can receive the conditioning Commons before the transmission network fails — the behaviors in ker are lost.

*Canonical instance:* Northern Aché. The FOXP2 circuit is intact. The hippocampal spatial system is intact. The Aché navigate their environment, maintain kinship networks, process social information normally. Only the acoustic cultural layer — specifically infant-directed song and communal dance — has collapsed. ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) ⊃ {lullaby, communal dance}. No register crossing was available: the colonial disruption severed the transmission network before any new substrate could receive the conditioning Commons.

**The Phase 3 ↔ Phase 2 branching condition:** The difference between a Phase 2 register crossing (CANTIO, Songline) and a Phase 3 collapse (Aché) is whether the prior transmission substrate remains operational for at least one full transmission cycle after the new substrate becomes available. For Luther: the oral Latin tradition was still functioning when the vernacular tradition emerged. For the Aché: the generation that knew the lullaby died or became socially inaccessible before the next generation could receive transmission. There was no register crossing because there was no new substrate ready. The Allee threshold was crossed catastrophically, not gradually.

**Songline current status (June 2026):** The Songline system is in Phase 2 (active maintenance, ρ₇ digital crossing underway) but approaching the Phase 2/3 boundary. The Warlpiri digital storybook (2025) is a preventive register crossing: the community is building the new substrate (ρ₇ digital) while the prior substrate (ρ₆ oral-GIS, elder custodians) is still functional. The race condition: can ρ₇ receive sufficient transmission before the custodian population falls below T1? The Mongabay reporting (May 2026) documents that Warlpiri elders are walking younger custodians along Songline tracks, verifying their accuracy — this is precisely the transmission event that the ρ₇ encoding is designed to preserve. The encoding is happening. The question is whether it is happening fast enough.

## Part VI — The Browning/Allee Distinction

CANTIO introduced the Browning ring as a metaphor for productive transformation: the acid of ker($F_{\rho_n}$) burns away the conditioning alloy that cannot survive the register crossing, leaving pure conditioning gold. HUMAN SONGS documented the Allee collapse: demographic acid that burns everything, including the gold.

The formal distinction, not previously named in either program:

**Browning transformation:** ker($F_{\rho_n}$) grows until the current substrate cannot hold the conditioning Commons. A new substrate $\rho_{n+1}$ is available. The conditioning Commons transfers to the new substrate; the elements that cannot transfer are in ker($F_{\rho_n}$) and are selectively lost. What survives is what the new substrate can hold: the pure conditioning gold. The "alloy" burned away is the substrate-specific encoding that was not portable to the new register.

$$\text{Browning condition: } \rho_{n+1} \text{ available when } \ker(F_{\rho_n}) \to \text{threshold}$$

**Allee collapse:** The transmission substrate $\rho_n$ fails before any $\rho_{n+1}$ is established. The conditioning Commons does not transfer — it disappears. What is lost is not the substrate-specific alloy but the conditioning gold itself: the song form, the behavioral capacity, the accumulated $X_{t-1}$ that enabled it.

$$\text{Allee condition: } \rho_{n+1} \text{ unavailable when } \rho_n \text{ fails; } \ker(F_{\text{acoustic}}) \text{ expands to } S$$

The key variable: **the overlap period between $\rho_n$ and $\rho_{n+1}$**. If the old substrate remains operational for at least one full transmission cycle after the new substrate becomes available, the conditioning Commons can register-cross. If the old substrate fails first, the Commons collapses.

Applied to the current Songline situation:
- Warlpiri elders (ρ₆ substrate: oral tradition, ceremonial knowledge, GIS-verified Songline routes) are alive and walking younger custodians along the tracks (Mongabay, May 2026)
- The ρ₇ digital storybook is being built while the ρ₆ substrate is still functional
- This is a Browning-condition crossing: ρ₇ available, ρ₆ still operational
- The Allee risk: if the rate of custodian mortality exceeds the rate of ρ₇ encoding, the crossing degrades toward Allee conditions

Applied to the Northern Aché:
- The colonial disruption created an Allee condition: the transmission network failed before any alternative substrate was established
- The acoustic proto-MVCC (lullaby) collapsed before the community could register-cross it into any new substrate
- The Browning metaphor is inapplicable: the acid destroyed the gold

The distinction is actionable. Any cultural institution currently in Phase 2 maintenance is running a race between Browning-condition and Allee-condition outcomes. The monitoring architecture (HUMAN SONGS, Part VII) is the instrument for detecting which condition is operative. If col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) is shrinking faster than the new substrate can receive encoding — if ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) is growing faster than any register crossing can capture — the system is trending Allee.

## Part VII — The Songline as Dual-Channel Fault-Tolerant MVCC System

SESAME COMMONS identified the Songline's fault-tolerant redundancy as multi-substrate (melody + rock art + kinship law + restricted ceremony). The dual-channel identification reveals a deeper layer: the redundancy operates not merely across social substrates but across neural channels.

The Songline simultaneously activates:

**Channel 1 — Spatial MVCC (hippocampal/entorhinal):**
The Songline route encodes navigational what/where conjunctions: this landscape feature (waterhole, rock formation, tree species, astronomical marker) at this location, with this directional relationship to the next feature. Each feature is a place-cell anchor; the route is a sequence of what/where conjunctions that populates col($F_{\text{spatial}}$) for the navigated country. A Warlpiri elder verifying that a younger custodian can accurately sing a Songline segment is verifying that the spatial MVCC is correctly populated in the younger person's hippocampal system.

**Channel 2 — Acoustic MVCC (FOXP2/corticostriatal):**

The cross-language melodic invariance of the Songline (SESAME COMMONS, Part IV) is the acoustic MVCC of the continental network — the minimum acoustic information that, when transmitted across language-group boundaries, allows spatial recombination in the new territory.

**Cross-channel fault tolerance:**
If Channel 1 fails (country inaccessible due to colonial restrictions, flooding, industrial development): Channel 2 preserves the route information in acoustic memory. The Songline can be reconstructed spatially from the melody when access is restored.

If Channel 2 fails (melodic transmission interrupted): Channel 1 preserves the route information in spatial memory. The places still encode the what/where conjunctions; the navigational knowledge survives even if the acoustic encoding of it is disrupted.

The Murujuga rock art (UNESCO World Heritage, 2025) is a secondary spatial-channel encoding: the petroglyphs at Murujuga encode Songline knowledge in a substrate that can survive centuries without active human maintenance. This is the spatial-channel analog of the ρ₇ digital storybook: a persistent encoding of Channel 1 information that can serve as recovery material if the primary spatial MVCC (living custodial knowledge) is disrupted.

**TH(songline, dual-channel):** The Songline's fault tolerance is not merely substrate-redundant (melody + rock art + kinship + ceremony). It is channel-redundant: the same navigational information is encoded in both the hippocampal spatial MVCC and the FOXP2/corticostriatal acoustic MVCC, using two independent neural systems that can each serve as recovery source for the other. This is a formally distinct and stronger form of fault tolerance than single-channel multi-substrate redundancy. It is the neural implementation of the BSMA Pillar 4 (Fault-Tolerant Redundancy) identified in SESAME COMMONS, now grounded in specific dual-channel neuroscience.

## Part VIII — FOXP2 Social Context as Individual-Scale col/ker Switch

The FoxP2 social-context regulation mechanism (Burkett et al., *eLife*, 2018; Miller et al., 2008) establishes a previously unnamed individual-scale instantiation of the col(F)/ker(F) partition:

- **FoxP2 high (female-directed singing):** col($F_{\text{vocal, individual}}$) is dominant. Song is stable, precise, reliably transmitted. The individual is in acoustic transmission mode.
- **FoxP2 low (undirected, solitary singing):** ker($F_{\text{vocal, individual}}$) expands. Song becomes variable, exploratory, less reliably transmitted. The individual is in acoustic exploration mode.

Social engagement — specifically, the presence of a receiver who triggers motivated, directed singing — is the switch between these states. The social transmission network does not merely provide social models (X_{t-1} supply); it also activates the neurobiological transmission mode (FOXP2 upregulation) that makes accurate acoustic reproduction possible.

This has a direct consequence for the HUMAN SONGS threshold framework:

**T1 (Human Vocal Allee Threshold)** is not merely a density threshold for social models. It is a threshold for social-context activation of FOXP2 transmission mode. Below T1, not only do children encounter fewer models — the adults who retain the behavior increasingly sing alone (in private, in mourning, in individual practice), which is the FoxP2-low, ker-dominant condition. The tradition is both socially under-transmitted and neurobiologically under-stabilized simultaneously.

The Aché case: no adult in the community models the lullaby in a caregiving social context (Singh & Hill, 2025). This means no Aché adult is in FoxP2-high transmission mode for the lullaby. Even if an Aché adult had partial lullaby knowledge — an incomplete X_{t-1} — they would not be singing it in a context that activates the neurobiological stabilization mechanism. The ker-expanding, FoxP2-low condition is the default. The col is not merely empty. The neurobiological mechanism for maintaining the col is socially deactivated.

**TH(FOXP2, col/ker switch):** The FoxP2/corticostriatal social-context regulation is the individual-scale acoustic col/ker switch. At population scale: the community's average FoxP2 transmission-mode activation rate for a given song form is proportional to the community's effective singer density for that form in relevant social contexts. A community below T1 for a specific song form is a community where that form's FoxP2 transmission mode is chronically suppressed — ker-dominant at individual scale, ker-growing at community scale. Monitoring col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) with the HUMAN SONGS Tier 1–4 architecture implicitly monitors the community's aggregate FoxP2 transmission-mode state for each song form.

The fault tolerance of the Songline is not merely multi-substrate (melody + rock art + kinship + ceremony). It is cross-channel (hippocampal spatial MVCC + FOXP2/corticostriatal acoustic MVCC), with each channel capable of serving as recovery source for the other. No other cultural institution in the three-program record implements cross-channel MVCC redundancy. This is why the Songline has maintained Phase 2 for 65,000 years while single-channel systems (the Aché lullaby, many folk traditions) have collapsed.

**Result 8 — FoxP2 social-context regulation is the individual-scale mechanism of the community-scale col/ker partition.**
The FoxP2 high/low switch (directed vs. undirected singing) is the individual instantiation of the col-dominant/ker-dominant acoustic state. Below T1 (Human Vocal Allee Threshold), the community is in chronic FoxP2-low, ker-dominant state for the relevant song forms: socially under-transmitted and neurobiologically under-stabilized simultaneously. Monitoring community col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) via the HUMAN SONGS Tier 1–4 architecture implicitly monitors the community's aggregate FoxP2 transmission-mode activation.

## Eight Falsifiable Predictions

**P1 — col/ker partition measurable across communities.**
Communities with higher singer density for specific song forms will show smaller ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) for those forms in PARADISEC archive vs. contemporary practice comparison. Specifically: communities with > 10 competent adult singers per 10 children for lullaby will show ker($F_{\text{acoustic, lullaby}}$) = ∅ (no archived-but-untransmitted lullaby forms). Communities below T1 will show growing ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) across successive archive recording decades.

*Falsification: A community below T1 showing ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) = ∅, or a community above T1 showing ker growth.*

**P2 — Perch 2.0 cross-channel transfer: spatial-to-acoustic.**
Perch 2.0 embeddings trained on bird vocalizations will achieve > 0.65 ROC-AUC on NHS Discography functional classification (lullaby/dance/healing/love) without human-music-specific fine-tuning (HUMAN SONGS, P2). Extension: the Perch 2.0 embeddings that successfully classify human vocal functions will cluster Aboriginal Australian Songline melodic segments by navigational vs. ceremonial function — demonstrating cross-domain transfer from bird song to human acoustic spatial encoding.

*Falsification: Perch 2.0 < 0.50 ROC-AUC on NHS Discography, or < chance on Songline functional classification.*

**P3 — Zipf's law holds in devotional acoustic corpora.**
The CANTIO claim (Zipf's law as the acoustic optimum of the devotional conditioning Commons) has not been directly tested. Application of Arnon et al. (2025) analytical framework to the full Lutheran chorale corpus, the full Psalm melodic corpus (in traditional cantillation), and the Wesley hymn corpus will reveal Zipfian melodic element frequencies with power-law exponent α ∈ [1.5, 2.5]. The most enduring hymns ("A Mighty Fortress," Psalm 23, "Amazing Grace") will show Zipfian fit closer to α = 2 than liturgically defunct or locally constrained hymns.

*Falsification: Non-Zipfian element-frequency distribution in hymn corpora with confirmed long-term oral transmission.*

**P4 — Lullaby loss precedes but predicts communal dance loss.**
Across communities experiencing gradual cultural disruption (not catastrophic bottleneck), the ordering of ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) expansion will follow the inverse McBride information-rate hierarchy: highest-complexity song forms enter ker first (ceremonial/healing songs), lullabies enter ker last, and communal dance enters ker simultaneously with or just before the lullaby. The Aché case is the catastrophic limit where both collapsed simultaneously. Gradual disruption will show the gradient.

*Falsification: A community showing communal dance collapse prior to lullaby collapse in gradual (non-catastrophic) disruption.*

**P5 — Cultural revitalization increases FoxP2 transmission-mode activation.**
Communities with active language-nest or cultural immersion programs (increased intergenerational cultural contact) will show measurable increase in song-form stability (decreased variability across performances) over 5–10 years of program operation, consistent with increased FoxP2 social-context transmission-mode activation. This is directly testable using the iterated-singing methodology of Anglada-Tort et al. (2023): record community singing at baseline and post-revitalization; measure variability across singers; compare communities with vs. without active transmission programs.

*Falsification: No measurable difference in song-form variability between revitalization and non-revitalization communities over a 10-year period.*

**P6 — Dual-channel encoding produces fault-tolerant spatial recall in human subjects.**
Subjects who learn a novel route through a landscape while singing a melody (dual-channel spatial + acoustic encoding) will show superior route reconstruction following single-channel disruption (melody-only recall cue, or route-only recall without melody) relative to subjects who learned the route through either channel alone. This is a direct experimental test of the Songline's dual-channel fault tolerance claim, implementable in standard spatial memory laboratory protocols.

*Falsification: Dual-channel encoding showing no recall advantage over single-channel encoding following single-channel disruption.*

**P7 — Warlpiri ρ₇ Browning condition verified 2026–2030.**
If the Warlpiri ρ₇ digital storybook constitutes a successful Browning crossing (new substrate available before old fails), then: by 2030, at least one Songline segment encoded in the ρ₇ storybook will be independently verified by a custodian who learned it from the digital encoding rather than from direct elder transmission — confirming that the ρ₇ substrate can receive and re-transmit Songline conditioning content. If the crossing fails (Allee condition develops), the generation that learned exclusively from digital encoding will show degraded accuracy in Songline route verification compared to the generation trained by living elder transmission.

*Falsification: No successful transmission from ρ₇ encoding to new custodian by 2030; or no degradation in hybrid vs. direct-transmission cohorts.*

**P8 — The Bhambore/Aché duality generalizes across vertebrates.**
If the MVCC is a property of X_{t-1} structure rather than brain size, then: among bird species with closed-ended vocal learning (regent honeyeater, as documented in Crates et al., 2021), social reintroduction of archived song models (the Appleby et al., 2023, tutor-song rescue) will restore col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) to pre-collapse levels within one breeding generation, while the FoxP2-low, ker-dominant state observed in socially isolated individuals will revert to FoxP2-high, col-dominant state upon social-context restoration. The X_{t-1} manipulation — not the biological capacity — determines the acoustic output.

*Falsification: Social reintroduction of song models fails to restore col($F_{\text{acoustic}}$) in biologically intact individuals; or FoxP2 upregulation absent in socially reintroduced individuals.*

## The Formal Invariants: Extended

The five invariants of SESAME COMMONS hold unchanged. Three additional invariants emerge from the three-program synthesis:

**Invariant 6 — The MVCC is seeded developmentally, not genetically.**
The lullaby is the acoustic proto-MVCC: the first social conditioning event that populates X_{t-1} for the FOXP2/corticostriatal system. Genetic specification provides the circuit (FOXP2/corticostriatal architecture, hippocampal place cell system). Social transmission populates the circuit's conditioning history. The Aché case is the proof: the gene is present, the circuit is present, the conditioning history is absent. $G_{\text{coord, acoustic}} = 0$.

**Invariant 7 — The MVCC lifecycle phase is determined by substrate overlap, not substrate quality.**
Whether a system is in Phase 2 (Maintenance, Browning crossing) or Phase 3 (Collapse, Allee failure) depends not on the quality of the new substrate ($\rho_{n+1}$) but on whether the old substrate ($\rho_n$) remains operational long enough to complete at least one full transmission cycle to the new substrate. The Warlpiri digital storybook is Phase 2 precisely because elders are still walking the Songlines while the encoding is underway. If the last custodian dies before the ρ₇ encoding is complete, the crossing degrades to Phase 3.

**Invariant 8 — Dual-channel MVCC systems are more fault-tolerant than single-channel systems by a mechanism independent of substrate redundancy.**
Single-channel multi-substrate redundancy (melody + rock art + kinship + ceremony) provides fault tolerance against substrate loss. Dual-channel redundancy (hippocampal spatial + FOXP2 acoustic) provides fault tolerance against channel disruption — different neural mechanisms carrying the same information. The Songline implements both levels of redundancy simultaneously. This is why it has remained in Phase 2 for 65,000 years across colonial disruption, geographic displacement, and transmission network stress that reduced single-channel traditions to Phase 3 collapse.

## Coda: The First Song and the Oldest Song

The lullaby is the first song any human hears. The Songline is the oldest continuously maintained song on Earth. They are the same formal object.

Enheduanna introduced the "I" into the devotional Commons 4,300 years ago. Before she could write it in clay, she had been conditioned by a lullaby. The FOXP2/corticostriatal circuit that would produce the first named author in human history was seeded by an unnamed singer in Ur — a mother or caregiver whose song is entirely in ker($F_{\text{history}}$), forever lost, but whose acoustic conditioning of that circuit is the precondition for everything CANTIO documents.

The bumblebee that rolled the ball under the flower on June 4, 2026 did not know the Songline. The bumblebee's brain weighs less than the clay fragment on which Enheduanna's hymn was first inscribed. The bumblebee solved the problem because its X_{t-1} contained two conditioning clauses that the novel situation recombined. The Songline custodians solving the navigational problem of crossing 2,300 km of continent without written maps used the same formal mechanism, accumulated across 65,000 years of intergenerational transmission, encoded in two neural channels simultaneously, fault-tolerant against any single failure mode.

The Northern Aché mother who does not sing to her child is not less musical than Enheduanna. She is not less cognitively capable than the bee. She is a human being whose X_{t-1, lullaby} was zeroed by colonial demographic catastrophe before she was born. The FOXP2 circuit waits. The social context that would activate it never arrives. The song form enters ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$).

This is what the three programs together establish: the acoustic conditioning Commons is not a luxury of large-brained species. It is not an artifact of cultural sophistication. It is a formal property of any system that stores partial conditioning information and has access to a social transmission network that activates the appropriate neural circuits.

The bee navigates. The ancestor sings. The mother holds her child in silence. The country is still the song. But somewhere, in ker($F_{\text{acoustic}}$), there are songs that no one is singing anymore.

The monitoring stack exists. The archives are being digitized. The elders are walking the tracks.

The question is whether we will build the encoding infrastructure before the silence reaches the continent.

$$G_{\text{coord}} = \sum_{t < s} I(a_t;\, a_s \mid X_{t-1}) > 0$$

The condition is not met by default.  
It is maintained by transmission.  
Or it falls silent.
